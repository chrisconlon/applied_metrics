\subsection*{1 Rating rounding: sharp, then fuzzy}

\emph{Based on Anderson and Magruder (2012), who use Yelp's half-star rounding.} A platform
computes each restaurant's average rating $r_{i}$ to two decimals but \emph{displays} it rounded to
the nearest half star. Restaurants with $r_{i} \ge 3.75$ display four stars; those just below display
three and a half. Simulate $3{,}000$ restaurants with $r_{i} \sim \text{Uniform}(3.25, 4.25)$, let
$x_{i} = r_{i} - 3.75$ be the distance to the threshold, and let log weekly revenue be
\[
y_{i} = 10 + 1.5\,x_{i} + 0.8\,x_{i}^{2} + 0.6\cdot\mathbf{1}\{x_{i} \ge 0\} + \varepsilon_{i},
\qquad \varepsilon_{i} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1).
\]
Underlying quality matters smoothly; the displayed star adds a jump of $0.6$ at the threshold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Naive comparison: mean $y$ for restaurants displaying four stars minus those displaying three and
a half. Why is this not the effect of the extra half star?
\item Binscatter: mean $y$ in $20$ equal-width bins of $x$; plot with a vertical line at zero.
\item Local linear RDD: regress $y$ on $\mathbf{1}\{x \ge 0\}$, $x$, and their interaction, using
restaurants within $h$ of the threshold, for $h = 0.5$, $0.25$, and $0.10$. Report the jump and its
robust standard error at each bandwidth. What is the tradeoff as $h$ shrinks, and how does the
$0.8\,x^{2}$ term in the truth show up in it?
\item Placebo cutoffs at $x = -0.25$ and $x = +0.25$, same bandwidth $0.25$. What should you find,
and what do you find?
\item Manipulation check: compare the number of restaurants just below and just above the threshold
(within $0.05$). Explain in one paragraph what a pile-up just above would suggest in the real
setting, and why it would invalidate the design.
\item Fuzzy version. Now the displayed star does not move revenue directly; instead it raises the
chance the platform \emph{features} the restaurant. Simulate an unobserved quality $q_{i} \sim
\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and
\[
F_{i} = \mathbf{1}\{-0.8 + 1.4\cdot\mathbf{1}\{x_{i} \ge 0\} + 0.8\,q_{i} + \nu_{i} > 0\},\quad
y_{i} = 10 + 1.5\,x_{i} + 0.8\,x_{i}^{2} + 1.0\,F_{i} + 0.8\,q_{i} + \varepsilon_{i},
\]
with $\nu_{i}, \varepsilon_{i} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. The effect of being featured is $1.0$.
Within $h = 0.25$: (i) regress $y$ on $F$ (and the running-variable terms); (ii) estimate the
reduced form and the first stage at the threshold; (iii) form the Wald ratio and compare to 2SLS
with the threshold dummy as the instrument for $F$. Why does (i) fail and (iii) work, and for
which restaurants is the 2SLS number the effect of being featured?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{2 A soda tax, one city, thirty donors}

A city imposes a soda tax at the start of quarter $13$ of a $20$-quarter panel; $30$ other cities do
not. Per-capita soda sales follow a factor structure,
\[
Y_{jt} = 100 + 5\left(\lambda_{1t}\,\mu_{j1} + \lambda_{2t}\,\mu_{j2}\right) + \varepsilon_{jt},
\qquad \varepsilon_{jt} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1),
\]
with $\lambda_{1t}$ a linear trend from $0$ to $2$ over the $20$ quarters, $\lambda_{2t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$
i.i.d.\ over $t$, and city loadings $\mu_{j1}, \mu_{j2} \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$ for the donors. Give the
treated city loadings $(0.9, 0.5)$: it is a fast-growing city. The tax cuts its sales by
$10\%$ in every post-tax quarter (multiply $Y_{1t}$ by $0.9$ for $t \ge 13$).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Simulate. Plot the treated city against the average of the donors. Estimate the effect by
difference-in-differences with all donors as the control group. Why is it biased here, and in which
direction?
\item Build the synthetic control: donor weights $w \ge 0$, $\sum_{j} w_{j} = 1$, chosen to minimize
the pre-period squared gap $\sum_{t \le 12}\big(Y_{1t} - \sum_{j} w_{j}Y_{jt}\big)^{2}$. (Eight-line
version: parameterize $w_{j} = e^{\theta_{j}}/\sum_{k} e^{\theta_{k}}$ and hand the objective to
\texttt{optim}; or use \texttt{quadprog::solve.QP}; or a package.) Report the weights above $0.05$,
the pre-period RMSPE, and the average post-period gap. Compare to the truth and to (a).
\item Why does the synthetic control get this right when DiD does not? Answer in terms of the factor
loadings.
\item Placebo in space: give the treatment to each donor in turn (with the treated city removed from
the donor pool), compute each donor's ratio of post- to pre-period RMSPE, and place the treated
city's ratio in that distribution. Report the implied $p$-value. What does this test assume?
\item Now move the treated city's loadings to $(1.3, 0.5)$, outside the range of the donors. Re-run
(b). What happens to the pre-period fit, and what does that tell you about when synthetic control
can and cannot be used?
\end{enumerate}
