\subsection*{1 Staggered adoption of a pricing tool}

$600$ firms observed for $16$ quarters. Firms adopt an algorithmic-pricing tool in one of three
cohorts, quarter $5$, $9$, or $13$, or never; assign each firm to one of the four groups at random
with equal probability. Log gross margin is
\[
y_{it} = \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{t} + \tau_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}, \qquad
\alpha_{i} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.3^{2}),\ \varepsilon_{it} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.05^{2}),
\]
with $\gamma_{t}$ a random walk with drift ($\gamma_{t} = \gamma_{t-1} + 0.01 + 0.02\,\zeta_{t}$)
common to all firms. The treatment effect ramps in, at a speed and to a level that differ by cohort:
\[
\tau_{it} = \bar\tau_{g}\cdot \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{k_{it} + 1}{s_{g}}\right) \text{ for } k_{it} = t - g \ge 0,
\quad \text{and } 0 \text{ before adoption,}
\]
where cohort $g = 5$ has $\bar\tau = 0.10$, $s = 1$ (full effect immediately); $g = 9$ has
$\bar\tau = 0.06$, $s = 2$; $g = 13$ has $\bar\tau = 0.03$, $s = 4$. Early adopters gain more
and faster. Never-treated firms have $\tau = 0$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Simulate. Compute the true average treatment effect on the treated: the mean of $\tau_{it}$
over treated firm-quarters (post-adoption). Also report it by cohort.
\item Estimate the static two-way fixed effects regression, $y$ on a post-adoption dummy with firm
and quarter fixed effects, clustered by firm. Compare to (a). Which cohorts is it under- or
over-weighting, and why? (Think about how many post-periods each cohort contributes.)
\item Estimate the naive event study: $y$ on event-time dummies (reference $k = -1$; never-treated
firms get the reference value) with firm and quarter fixed effects. Plot it. Run a joint test on the
pre-period coefficients. They should reject even though no firm has a pre-trend. Explain, in the
language of Session 9, what contaminates the pre-period coefficients here.
\item Re-estimate with \texttt{sunab(cohort, quarter)} (Sun and Abraham 2021), plot, and compare the
post-period coefficients to the true average of $\tau_{it}$ at each event time. Report the aggregated
ATT and compare to (a).
\item Drop the never-treated firms and re-run (d). What does \texttt{sunab} use as the control group
now, and which event-time coefficients become unavailable? One paragraph.
\item Managerial summary, three sentences: the analytics team reports the static two-way FE number
from (b) as ``the effect of the tool.'' What is wrong with it, what number would you report instead,
and to whom does it apply?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{2 Goodman-Bacon by hand}

Two cohorts of equal size and three periods. The \emph{early} cohort is treated in periods $2$ and
$3$; the \emph{late} cohort is treated in period $3$ only. There is no never-treated group. Potential
outcomes: $y_{it}(0) = \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{t}$, and the treatment effect for a unit treated for
$k + 1$ periods is $\tau(k)$, so the early cohort has effect $\tau(0)$ in period $2$ and
$\tau(1)$ in period $3$, and the late cohort has effect $\tau(0)$ in period $3$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Write the population mean of $y$ for each cohort-period cell (six cells) in terms of
$\bar\alpha_{g}$, $\gamma_{t}$, and $\tau(\cdot)$.
\item There are two $2\times 2$ difference-in-differences comparisons available. Comparison $A$ uses
periods $1$--$2$ with the late cohort (not yet treated) as the control for the early cohort.
Comparison $B$ uses periods $2$--$3$ with the \emph{early} cohort (already treated) as the control
for the late cohort. Compute what each estimates in terms of $\tau(0)$ and $\tau(1)$. One of them
is not a treatment effect. Which, and what is it instead?
\item Show that the two-way fixed effects regression of $y$ on the treatment dummy with unit and
period effects estimates $\tfrac{1}{2}A + \tfrac{1}{2}B$ in this design. (Hint: with equal cohort
sizes and this timing, the within-unit-and-period residualized treatment dummy takes only a few
values; the TWFE coefficient is a variance-weighted average of the $2\times 2$s, and here the two
comparisons have equal variance. You may argue from symmetry rather than grinding the algebra.)
\item Let $\tau(0) = 0.5$ and $\tau(1) = 1.0$: the effect grows the longer a firm has the tool.
What does TWFE estimate? Compare it to every true effect in the design. Now let $\tau(1) = 1.5$.
\item Simulate the design ($100$ firms per cohort, small noise) and confirm (c) and (d): compute
$A$ and $B$ from the six cell means and compare their average to \texttt{feols(y \~{} D | id + t)}.
\item One paragraph: what feature of the design makes comparison $B$ appear, why is it harmless
when $\tau(k)$ is constant in $k$, and what do Callaway--Sant'Anna and Sun--Abraham do about it?
\end{enumerate}
